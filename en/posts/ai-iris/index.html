Can AI-Restored Iris Photos Fool Iris Recognition?

2026-10-04 09:15 UTC

An experimental record of verifying the biometric fidelity of AI image restoration across three iris recognition algorithms.

Origins

I took several macro photos of my own eyes, wanting a nice set of iris close-ups. However, common issues with smartphone macro photography arose—skewed angles, harsh flash glare reflecting across the pupil, and suboptimal resolution. I decided to use AI to restore and enhance the images. Visually, the output looked remarkably impressive to the naked eye.

That immediately raised a question: Does an AI-restored iris image still retain its original biometrics at the algorithmic level? In other words, can it pass iris recognition?

This question carries security implications in two distinct directions:

  • If it can pass—posting an AI-enhanced close-up of your eyes on social media might inadvertently leak your iris biometric identity.
  • If it cannot pass—attempting to synthesize fake irises with AI to attack access control systems is, at least under current technology, unfeasible.

To rigorously address this question, I designed three levels of experiments.

The core distinction between ai 1 and ai 2 lies in the depth of AI intervention: the former focuses on “enhancement” and preserves most raw structural features; the latter undergoes substantial repainting, completely synthesizing over the glare regions.

Experimental Design

Three algorithms evaluated the images from different perspectives, spanning classical computer vision to modern deep learning:

  1. Classical Daugman Algorithm: Rubber sheet unwrapping + Log-Gabor filter encoding + Hamming Distance (HD). Implemented from scratch, performing a spectral sweep across multiple filter wavelengths.
  2. Worldcoin IRIS Production Pipeline: Open-source open-iris v1.0.0, utilizing MobileNetV2 segmentation + perspective normalization + multi-scale Gabor encoding. This represents algorithms deployed in production hardware.
  3. DINOv2 Deep Features: Meta’s self-supervised ViT model, extracting dense patch-level feature embeddings for cosine similarity evaluation.

All samples required geometric alignment. Because the shooting angles of the raw captures and AI processing differed, we unified the coordinate space using SIFT feature matching + RANSAC homography estimation, subsequently mapping the annotated pupil/iris contours back to the raw coordinate space.

In brief:

  • The AI-enhanced image (ai 1) achieved HD=0.252 under Worldcoin’s industrial pipeline, successfully passing verification. It also came close to passing under the low-frequency band (λ=64px) of the Daugman algorithm.
  • The AI deeply-inpainted image (ai 2) failed to pass conclusively under any method, and was directly rejected by geometry validation within Worldcoin’s pipeline.
  • The negative control exhibited solid stability, with all methods correctly rejecting an unrelated eye.

Daugman Multi-Band Sweep: The Most Intriguing Finding

During the Daugman experiment, I swept the full spectrum from λ=4 to λ=64 pixels. The findings were telling:

  • High-frequency band (λ≤8px): ai 1 yielded a Hamming distance around 0.49, indistinguishable from random noise. The micro-fibrous textures generated by AI are mathematically fabricated.
  • Low-frequency band (λ≥32px): ai 1 dropped to approximately 0.30 HD, entering the match threshold. AI preserved the macro-anatomical architecture of the iris—crypts, collarette, and pigment distributions.

This demonstrates that the AI performed two distinct tasks during restoration: it recovered macroscopic structures degraded by flash glare (which are authentic), while synthesizing an overlay of convincing yet stochastically generated micro-textures (which are synthetic).

Because Worldcoin’s Gabor filters operate in the mid-frequency range, ai 1 passed—the algorithm primarily assesses macro structures that were authentically preserved.

Worldcoin IRIS: ai 2 Betrayed by Being “Too Round”

An intriguing detail: before reaching feature comparison, ai 2 was intercepted by the eyeball eccentricity detection module in Worldcoin’s pipeline.

When repainting, the AI rendered the pupil into a near-perfect circle (eccentricity 0.0041). However, real human eyes invariably exhibit slight ellipticity due to viewing angles and corneal curvature (eccentricity threshold ≥ 0.1). The pipeline flagged this “overly perfect circle” as artificial and aborted the process.

This highlights an ironic reality: AI’s aesthetic obsession with perfection became its algorithmic flaw.

DINOv2: Semantic-Level Discrimination

As a foundation vision model, DINOv2 assigned high similarity scores (0.82–0.97) across all iris pairs at the CLS token (global feature) level, because it categorized them all as “human eyes.”

However, dense patch-level features provided meaningful discrimination: 0.847 for ground truth, dropping to 0.696 for ai 1, 0.646 for ai 2, and 0.588 for the negative control. The stratification was clear and consistent with expectations.

Limitations

To remain transparent, several constraints must be noted:

  1. Very small sample size: Only one pair of eyes (5 images). Formal academic conclusions require validation on large-scale datasets; this serves as an exploratory personal experiment.
  2. Workaround in the Worldcoin pipeline: Heavy glare in the raw captures caused segmentation failures. We reconstructed segmentation contours via morphological operations, partially bypassing quality control gates. The results reflect “matching capability given accurate segmentation” rather than an “end-to-end consumer experience.”
  3. Approximated circle parameters for the negative control: Rough estimations were used for pupil/iris localization on other. Since it solely served to verify that “different eyes are rejected,” high precision was unnecessary.
  4. DINOv2 is not an iris-specialized model: Its evaluations lean toward semantic similarity rather than biometric identity verification.

Conclusion

Returning to the initial question: Can AI-restored iris photos fool iris recognition?

The answer depends on what the AI did:

  • Enhancement / Super-resolution / Light denoising (like ai 1): Macroscopic biometrics are substantially preserved. Reaching HD=0.252 in an industrial pipeline, it can pass verification.
  • Deep repainting / Extensive inpainting (like ai 2): Generates extensive synthetic textures with artificially “perfect” geometry, failing verification.

For average users, this implies: Sharing AI-enhanced high-resolution eye photos on social media does indeed carry your real iris biometric information. Whether that represents an immediate security threat depends on whether an adversary can spoof liveness detection using a 2D photograph—which remains an entirely separate challenge.

Test environment: Windows + Python 3.10, utilizing Worldcoin open-iris v1.0.0, OpenCV SIFT, and PyTorch DINOv2 vits14. All code and images were processed strictly on local hardware; no biometric data was uploaded to any remote server.
